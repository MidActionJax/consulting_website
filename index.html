// THE PROBLEM

The failures that don't look like failures

The expensive problems in research software are rarely the crashes. They are the jobs that log a success and produce nothing, the numbers nobody can reconstruct six months later, and the system that only runs because one person remembers how it works.

That is the work I'm hired for: finding where a system fails quietly, then building the monitoring, automation and provenance that keep it honest.

  • Silent failures

    Jobs report success while the output is missing or wrong.

  • Unreproducible results

    No one can rerun the analysis that produced the figure.

  • One-person systems

    The pipeline lives in one engineer's head.

// FOR SBIR TEAMS

The technical side of your proposal

Most proposal consultants are strong on narrative and commercialization. I'm the technical side: the data management plan, the software sustainability section, and the computational approach, written by an engineer who builds these systems and reads them the way a technical reviewer will.

I also work with proposal firms on a subcontract or white-label basis, and with state programs, SBDCs and I-Corps hubs that refer companies to outside technical help.

Some SBIR and STTR awardees can use Technical and Business Assistance (TABA) funds for outside technical help. Eligibility and amounts depend on the agency and the award, so check your solicitation.
